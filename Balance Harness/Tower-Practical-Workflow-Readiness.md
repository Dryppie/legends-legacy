## Prospective precision check

The original assessment sketched a future ceiling of three restarts, six selected outputs plus two anchors, and at most 1,024 paired confirmation trials per distinct recipe. It explicitly left precision and the interval method unresolved. This calculation evaluates one transparent extension of the current gate; it does not adopt a protocol or alter the current seven-quantity practical gate.

The [reproducible script](analysis/practical-confirmation-precision.py) uses only Python's standard library and the checked-in 256-outcome fixture. [All assumptions, checks and numerical results](Tower-Practical-Confirmation-Precision.json) are saved separately. It neither reads historical evaluation-seed values nor draws random numbers.

For one fixed candidate/reference pair, let `q` be the probability that their win/non-win outcomes differ and `delta` the candidate's true win-rate gain. Under an IID paired-outcome model, gain, loss and equal-outcome probabilities are `(q + delta)/2`, `(q - delta)/2` and `1 - q`. The script sums the multinomial distribution numerically, using the same Wilson-bound arithmetic as the harness. A contrast passes only when the observed gain is at least 0.05 and `WilsonLower(gains) - WilsonUpper(losses) > 0`.

For the hypothetical future study, a family size of **26** is assumed: eight recipe-rate quantities plus gain/loss quantities for nine predeclared comparisons (three candidate restarts, each versus two anchors and its same-restart comparator). This is one conservative prospective choice, not a previously approved interval family. Exact duplicate recipes do not shrink this planning family after outcomes are seen.

The pilot fixture has discordances of **102/256 = 39.84%** and **126/256 = 49.22%**. These describe two past pairs and are sensitivity inputs, not forecasts for an unknown candidate or comparator. With 1,024 trials and the assumed 26-quantity family:

| Assumed discordance | One contrast passes at a true +5 pp | One contrast passes at a true +10 pp |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 10% sensitivity case | 48.6% | approximately 100% |
| 39.84%, pilot second pair | 8.1% | 87.9% |
| 49.22%, pilot first pair | 6.2% | 77.8% |
| 60% sensitivity case | 5.4% | 68.0% |

These are **single necessary-contrast probabilities**, excluding the viability requirement, other required comparisons, search/selection uncertainty and the full multi-restart decision. They are not joint study power or method reliability. The three restarts also share confirmation conditions, so their success probabilities must not simply be treated as independent.

There is nevertheless a useful conditional bound: if each of three restarts has at least one required contrast with pass probability no more than 8.053%, then the probability of two or more successful restarts is at most `3 * 0.0805305 / 2 = 12.1%`. This follows because two successes require the count to be at least two; it does not assume independence between restarts. Under those assumptions, the proposed ceiling cannot meet an 80% overall target for a true five-point gain.

There is a second design issue: the gate requires an **observed** gain of at least five points. If the true gain is exactly five points, that threshold lies near the center of the sampling distribution. As sample size grows, its pass probability approaches roughly one half under the stated nondegenerate model, even when the positive-confidence-bound requirement becomes easy. Increasing the sample alone therefore does not solve an 80%-power target defined at that same boundary. A future design must distinguish the minimum observed gain from the true alternative at which power is required. The +10-point column is sensitivity analysis, not approval to change the target.
