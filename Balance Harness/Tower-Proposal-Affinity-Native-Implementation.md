## Native execution and evidence

`TowerProposalRacingNative.RunAsync` accepts an already admitted, captured, exclusively owned `TowerLoadoutArchive`. Before dispatch it requires an unused archive with an exact 528-trial limit, zero cache hits, matching saved scope, supported report storage, captured settings/content and the current execution identity. Its archive algorithm includes the entire proposal-plan hash, so a different policy, inventory or root cannot reuse the archive. Each panel additionally has its own cache namespace.

The adapter hashes the captured data files, rebuilds the inventory through the production catalog, compares the **entire** inventory with the v2 evidence, and reproduces the generation mechanics. Matching source-hash labels alone cannot authenticate invented or changed typed nodes. Version 1 remains supported without inventing affinity evidence.

The existing 528-trial controller still owns generation, pruning, carried beam members and selection. Both waves use actual evaluator feedback; there is no precomputed second wave. The adapter persists the plan, candidate batches and complete panel freezes before dispatch, then durable attempt charges and materialized-input identities before each fight. It authenticates returned trial identity, cache/input hash, seed, recipe, outcome and duration. Invalid or cancelled attempts retain their charges and partial evidence without a selected output. Resource checks and a bounded, create-new evidence writer remain mandatory; retries and resumption are rejected.

`VerifyAsync` requires the archive's external `files.json` SHA-256 pin. It verifies the file inventory, captured content and runtime, reconstructs the actual proposal trajectory from literal saved observations, rebuilds every prepared input identity and checks it against archived battle reports. Batches, five panel freezes, charge and input journals, recipe/battle inventories and final selection must agree. It verifies the inventory and external pin again before returning. Verification executes zero fights, although verification of a real native archive materializes inputs. No real native archive was created in this implementation turn.

`TowerProposalComparison.RunPairAsync` binds a pair to its frozen prospective design, requires separate non-nested archives, and preflights **both** before running the control. The candidate runs only if the control completes. Incomplete pairs remain failures without replacement. Overall process ownership, admission, all-root freezing, held-out evaluation, final inventories and independent publication audits belong to the future enclosing controller; this adapter is not a campaign launcher.

## Why this comparison has two arms

The retained generation review changed only five of 108 first-wave candidate positions across all twelve historical roots; seven first waves were identical. Those observations motivated a focused experiment, not selection of the five differing roots. First-wave equality does not establish second-wave equality or equal selected outputs.

Both proposed arms use v2, the same benchmark parent, nine/eight single-edit schedules, the same random stream convention and the unchanged racing/selection kernel. The control explicitly selects no damage affinities. The candidate selects the three reviewed Poison routes: the two Spiderling paths and Royal Venom, each paired with Viper. Their union preserves three existing essence assignments on benchmark owner 8. Other interaction preservation remains off. Names and evidence identities differ; the only proposal-behavior change is that explicit preservation selection.

There is no extra legacy search arm. The original three reference teams remain available inside each search, including the existing positive-tie primary `399bc776…`. The held-out benchmark is the fixed confirmed team `96b943571150684df3a5be5352c94d60b32485b5797bb7b763b74f73faead78c`. The comparison isolates the generator constraint; it does not also change tie preference or promote the benchmark to a different selector role.

| Total fight ceiling | **21,888** |
| Scientific resource ceiling | **10,800 seconds / 6 GiB**, including verification and publication |

`Bind` accepts the complete ordered allocation, never draws it, and rejects duplicates, wrong counts and intersections with the supplied historical exclusions, old generation roots, reference seeds and legacy schedules. The first 876 values are twelve contiguous 73-value search blocks; the remaining values are twelve 256-value held-out blocks. Both arms get the exact same racing context and search panels. Every other root's values and every held-out panel are forbidden to that search. Historical ledger completeness and actual reservation ownership require admission; the binder cannot authenticate facts absent from its inputs.

A future allocator should use the established single bounded 16,384-word entropy batch, select the first 3,948 acceptable distinct values in order, reserve every fresh exposed tail value, and never refill. No values have been selected here. The synthetic integer ranges in unit tests are fixture data, not scientific allocations.

Before observing any held-out result, the owner must freeze all 24 outputs and their exact physical recipes. Within each root's held-out panel, identical physical recipes are measured once and credited to all matching roles. An identical control/candidate output contributes an exact zero method contrast, while its absolute result and comparison with the benchmark are still measured. This can reduce physical held-out cost but never the denominator of twelve roots. No search is screened out, stopped for lack of differentiation, retried or replaced. A failed root invalidates the complete-study comparison; technical evidence and reservations remain retained.

The twelve-root size is a bounded development pilot, not a power calculation. Five historical changed candidate positions are neither five independent effect estimates nor an estimate of the future selected-output effect. More held-out trials would improve conditional measurement but would not manufacture more independent search roots. This plan deliberately reports limited differentiation rather than paying for another broad arm or selecting favorable roots.

## Endpoints and interpretation

For root `r`, let `d[r]` be the paired held-out win-rate difference between candidate and control, and `g[r]` the difference between candidate and fixed benchmark. The primary endpoint is the equally weighted mean of all twelve `d[r]`; the benchmark guardrail is the mean of all twelve `g[r]`. Wins use the existing content-outcome definition. Report every root, selected recipe, changed candidate positions in each wave, identical selected outputs, absolute win rates, paired gains/losses and novelty relative to all three retained references.

Root summaries include mean, median, worst root, sample spread and the descriptive two-sided t interval with eleven degrees of freedom (`mean ± 2.20098516 × sample SD / sqrt(12)`). With twelve roots and potentially many exact ties, this is a small-sample approximation, not calibrated efficacy evidence. Zero observed spread or identical outputs do not prove equality over future roots. Paired seed standard errors and method/benchmark covariance describe uncertainty conditional on these frozen outputs; they must remain distinct from root-to-root variation.

The plan freezes development decisions in this order:

1. Any incomplete root: technical failure, no complete-study efficacy conclusion.
2. `AbandonThisConfiguration` if the method mean is at most −2 percentage points, or the benchmark mean is at most −2 points with fewer than three promising novel outputs.
3. If all selected control/candidate recipes are identical: `NoObservedOutputDifferentiation`.
4. `LargerFreshEvaluationWarranted` only if the method mean is at least +2 points, the benchmark mean is nonnegative, at least three roots have differing selected outputs, and at least three novel candidate outputs gain at least +3 points over the benchmark.
5. Otherwise: `Inconclusive`.

These are prospective engineering thresholds, not significance tests or adoption rules. They neither authorize extension of this pilot nor promote a search policy or exact team. Independent future evaluation remains necessary.
