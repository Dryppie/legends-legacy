# Loadout placement: owned process failure cleanup

Current status: owned process cleanup preserves primary failures, attempts every acquired handle close once and shares a single drain/root-wait allowance. Hard external/transient storage, monitor/caller memory and complete observer/cleanup lifetime remain unresolved. Both compressed guards and the 1,806/1,800-second gate stay closed.

The target is the offline Balance Harness. Review of the previous job-limit implementation found a concrete failure-path defect in `build/bounded_windows_process.py`: one failed handle close could prevent the remaining closes, including the job close; secondary cleanup or observer errors could replace the original failure; and termination/drain and root waiting could each receive a fresh cleanup allowance. This step fixes those defects before attempting broader resource isolation. It does not introduce a disk quota or filesystem confinement mechanism.

The helper now attempts each acquired thread, process and job handle close exactly once, even after earlier termination, log-finalization, diagnostic or close failures. The original body exception remains primary. If the body succeeded, the first cleanup failure is raised; later failures are attached as notes. An observer callback failure follows the same rule, after the handle-close attempts. The helper and bounded-log pipe context track exceptions raised by their own bodies explicitly. An already-handled exception in the caller cannot mark a successful run failed or suppress a new handle/pipe close failure. Expected unavailable memory/I/O queries retain their existing unknown-coverage behavior.

One monotonic deadline begins before termination or the normal root wait. Termination, drain polling and root waiting share that allowance. The root wait receives the remaining whole milliseconds, rounded down, or zero when exhausted. Failed termination/drain is not retried with another allowance. `WAIT_FAILED` reports the Windows error separately from a timeout. This is a limit on requested waits, not a hard wall-clock guarantee for kernel calls, scheduling, diagnostics or the enclosing observer.

Windows documents that closing a process handle does not itself terminate that process and generally calls for one close per acquired handle; see [CloseHandle](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/handleapi/nf-handleapi-closehandle). Job termination also covers nested jobs; see [TerminateJobObject](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/jobapi2/nf-jobapi2-terminatejobobject). A zero-millisecond wait is a poll, and supported Windows versions exclude low-power time from the wait timeout; see [WaitForSingleObject](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/synchapi/nf-synchapi-waitforsingleobject). These semantics do not establish successful drain after an unverified fallback close.

New observations include the additive `tower-owned-process-cleanup-v1` block. It records `NotAcquired`, `Closed` or `CloseFailed` for each handle, secondary errors, the shared wait policy and the boundary after close attempts but before callback invocation. The common owner ledger validates the states and rejects cleanup errors without an owner failure. Historical observations without the block remain readable. `includesHandleCleanup` means that close attempts are included, not that all closes necessarily succeeded. Closing the job after a failed explicit termination leaves `jobDrained` false unless drain was independently confirmed; unknown job I/O remains unknown.

The saved cleanup record excludes its observer callback. A later callback failure is propagated or attached to the primary exception, without rewriting the earlier observation as if it covered its own delivery. `wallClockBounded`, `wholeProcessCoverage` and `usableForAdmission` remain false. Failed termination or close can still leave unresolved ownership; in particular, a failed termination of an unassigned suspended root cannot be repaired by closing an unrelated job handle.
