# Supported affinity search and one bounded nomination experiment

The working baseline is the original benchmark affinity-creation proposer (`tower-proposal-policy-v3`) with benchmark validation (`tower-proposal-racing-v5`). This is a supported engineering choice, not a claim of statistical superiority. The existing confirmed benchmark remains the recommendation until a challenger independently qualifies.

The one permitted algorithm experiment changes only which nominee may challenge the benchmark. Control selects among the four nonbenchmark nominees. Candidate selects among the two newly generated finalists, leaving the three reference teams as measured controls. Nomination uses the existing win-count, zero-win health and frozen-order rules. Both arms retain identical generation, screening, panels, 528-fight budgets and the exact 60-pair validation gate. Failed validation returns the benchmark; incomplete execution returns no recommendation.

This targets observed reference displacement without fitting a score margin or weakening validation. It can also hurt when an older reference is actually stronger in another context. It is therefore an explicit experimental profile, not the supported default.

The comparison retains twelve paired roots, 256 held-out seeds per distinct output/reference, all 24 outputs frozen before held-out evaluation, at most 21,888 fights, 10,800 seconds and 6 GiB. There is one scientific attempt, no replacements, resampling, retries or follow-up variants in this cycle. Fresh values must come from the existing admitted owner. Earlier experimental seeds remain excluded.

The predeclared advancement rule is at least +2 percentage points against control, at least +2 points against the benchmark, at least three differing roots and three novel outputs each gaining at least three points against the benchmark. Either endpoint at or below -2 points abandons the configuration. All other complete outcomes leave the supported baseline in place and end this tuning cycle. Passing warrants a separately justified confirmation; it does not automatically promote a default or prove general search reliability.

After this cycle, prioritize practical operation, runtime and representative encounter coverage. Do not continue inventing variants on this floor merely because a result is inconclusive.
