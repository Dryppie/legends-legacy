# Why the small composition-only search missed the controls

15 September 2026. Completed analysis of the sealed pilot's **50 proposals, 48 measured teams and 480 character loadouts**, using saved evidence only. **Zero fights, replays, preparations, generated candidates or new seed values.** All **482,371 reservations** and **1159 indexed files** across the pilot preparation, study and execution packages are preserved.

**The observed gap is assembly of useful-looking combinations, before selection.** All 80 eligible Essences appeared somewhere, and every control ingredient was legal and reachable. However, no generated character contained even three of the controls' four shared ingredients; none contained the complete shared set or any of the controls' ten distinct five-Essence loadouts. The source and recorded trajectory agree on ranking, nomination, parent references and module-library contents. This identifies a coverage gap, not proof that copying those combinations would create a strong team.

The analysis does not isolate shallow search from insufficient construction guidance. Only 48 candidates were measured on four discovery seeds. The historical deep search did sometimes assemble the common four-set, but used a different policy/stream/order scope and cannot supply an equal-input comparison for this pilot. It also failed recovery. Do not assume that either more depth or this proposed construction improvement is sufficient.

Across all 480 generated loadouts, **zero contain any three of those ingredients**, zero contain all four, and zero match any of the controls' ten complete loadout sets. The primary uses some of these ingredients on different characters; that does not assemble their same-owner combination. Both finalists have no Royal Venom.

Pack Howler plus Venomous Spiderling occurred together on **19 owner positions across 13 teams**, while Howler plus Royal Venom occurred on **one owner in one team**. Neither pair grew into their three-Essence union anywhere in this pilot. These counts are adaptive candidate observations, not independent combat samples or causal synergy tests.

## Retention and selection checks

The separate recount verifies **16 exact library hashes, 41 recorded module uses and 49 earlier-parent references**. Libraries were reconstructed from each completed prefix using the frozen four-leader ranking and 128-module capacity. All 50 chronological four-leader lists also agree. Every actual source module belonged to an earlier measured team; controls never entered generation. Primary analysis additionally checks the non-loadout parent choices against the beam/exploration pool and the exact operator schedule.

There were **243 distinct generated character loadouts**. None of the controls' ten loadouts existed in those generated sets or in any recorded library. Consequently, eviction of an already assembled control loadout does not explain this pilot's gap. This does not establish that every useful non-control module was retained or that a different library policy could never help. Loadouts inherit their source team's ranking; no individual loadout fitness was measured.

The top-four screen and top-two finalist selections reconstruct exactly. Every screen result was 0/16, so the frozen tie-break retained discovery order. No discovery-winning candidate was omitted: there were no discovery wins. Unknown strength of unscreened or unconfirmed candidates remains unknown. The pilot's already verified confirmation remains **0/64 and 0/64 for the finalists**, versus **7/64 and 4/64 for the controls**, with all four adjusted paired intervals spanning zero.

## What the generator recognizes

Saved metadata contains **48 mechanic groups**: eight basic-attack pairs, nine chains and 31 condition groups. It already includes both Howler–Spiderling and Howler–Royal Venom as same-owner structural hypotheses. It contains no group joining those three together. Such metadata explicitly leaves actual target, timing, chance, conditions, cooldowns and action blocking unverified.

The frozen fresh-construction path chooses one provider per coverage category and a random owner count, reserves those slots, then tries one compatible small mechanic group on approximately half the characters before filling spare slots. It does not systematically join overlapping groups into a larger loadout. Mutation and whole-loadout reuse must discover the larger combination through subsequent sampling.

There is also a concrete difference between the two hint systems. Pack Howler is one of five recognized attack-enabler providers, and is present in the mechanic groups, but has **no filler intent labels**. Consequently its filler weight is one, whereas a matching intent can receive seven or eight. Royal Venom has pressure/denial/sustain capability labels and mechanic links, but its direct-effect coverage category is recovery. It is one of 17 recovery providers. The coverage extractor examines direct ability effects; a poison interaction found through a status is not necessarily represented in that direct coverage category. Neither Essence is globally missing from metadata.

The archived fresh-construction intent text records category counts, but not the selected provider ID or each attempted core insertion. Therefore these records cannot attribute an individual missing combination to a particular random provider draw, slot conflict or skipped core. They establish incomplete assembly coverage and uneven hint representation, not the exact causal weight of either mechanism.

## Recommended engineering target

Implement a **bounded way to join compatible mechanic groups that share an Essence**, using only content-derived relationships. For example, the generic rule should be able to join A+B and A+C into A+B+C before filling a character's remaining slots. It should work across the full eligible pool, respect family/ownership limits, deduplicate canonical sets, retain the fixed ability order and preserve a route to other legal compositions. Then the existing distribution/mutation mechanisms can explore owner counts and the remaining ingredients.

Start with zero-combat fixtures proving that overlapping groups are combined deterministically, illegal unions are rejected, permutations do not consume separate candidates, and no control recipe or outcome enters generation. Record selected group IDs and insertion outcomes so a later failure can be diagnosed. Keep this an explicit new policy, with historical semantics/caps intact. This is a proposed engineering step, **not an implemented optimizer or a measured strength improvement**. Do not hardcode the four named Essences, import control teams, tune ability order or launch a larger unchanged fight batch based on this audit. The relative merits of joining groups, changing filler hints or increasing depth remain unmeasured.
