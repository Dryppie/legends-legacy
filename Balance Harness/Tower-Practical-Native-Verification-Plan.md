**Completed engineering prerequisite:** the [handoff implementation and verification review](Tower-Practical-Allocation-Handoff-Review.md) closes the missing connection identified during the initial inspection. `tower-practical-search-allocation-check` and `tower-practical-search-allocate-run` accept a versioned unscheduled template and allocation contract. See the [public contract](../LL/tools/BalanceHarness/PRACTICAL-SEARCH.md#owned-allocation-and-launch), [allocator](../LL/tools/BalanceHarness/TowerPracticalAllocation.cs) and [launcher/registration](../LL/tools/BalanceHarness/TowerPracticalSearchRun.cs).

The initial boundary remains relevant: a private binder cannot publish a panel externally and then ask the declared-input command to ignore the collision, nor pre-create its intended output. The new route creates the output once and allocates inside the existing watched worker while its parent holds the registry/output leases. Full-history checks exclude only that owned output. Search, native verification, deadlines and cumulative accounting remain shared with the declared-input route.

The implemented guarantees are:

* Freeze the unscheduled template, intended output, producing identity, allocation domain/master and allowances before derivation. Hold the registry lease from history reconciliation through allocation and publication.
* Publish Pending before deriving a value. Durably record each start and completed candidate; interrupted derivations or partial panels remain blocking. Reconstruct all recorded results and the complete binding before execution/publication/audit.
* Bind the final definition and Complete reservation before combat. Every accepted value remains excluded after completion, including unused panels. Allocated-search Pending has no supported recovery format yet and remains blocking.
* Preserve the original deadline, prior/setup/byte charges and failed derivation/attempt records. Existing or abandoned outputs cannot launch again. Historical receipt formats and the declared-input contract are unchanged.

The 43 added zero-combat checks cover pending/start/partial/full-panel interruptions, worker-start failure, normal handoff, actual parent death, deadlines/storage stops, competing ownership, history drift/capacity and semantic tampering. A second allocator sees either the entire completed panel or a blocking unresolved claim. Worker startup now precedes allocation, so a startup failure has no derived values to recover. Literal synthetic inputs establish these engineering boundaries; they do not establish native gameplay success.

The 600-second proposal below is a prospective execution envelope for the implemented route. It includes allocation inside the run phase and is not an allowance for repairing the adapter during a real allocation. Its scope has not been authorized by the engineering follow-up.
