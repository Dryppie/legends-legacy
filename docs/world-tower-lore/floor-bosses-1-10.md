# World Tower Floor Bosses: Floors 1–10

> Status: Concept lore and encounter-design reference. The bosses currently exist, but the shared narrative and any proposed naming changes are not yet implemented canon.

## The Court of the Broken Crown

Floors 1–10 form **The Court of the Broken Crown**: a climb through the ruined institutions, memories, and lies of the Mad King's kingdom.

Each floor reveals part of the kingdom's collapse:

- Floors 1–4 introduce the court and its corruption.
- Floor 5 is the first major test of ascension.
- Floors 6–8 reveal the tragedy that broke the King.
- Floor 9 presents several conflicting versions of the truth.
- Floor 10 confronts the man who turned tragedy into tyranny.

The existing bosses remain in their current order. Their mechanics already form a strong learning curve; this concept gives them a shared narrative.

## Floor 1 — The Waking Step

### Garran, the Gatekeeper

Garran was the first soldier to swear himself to the crown and the last to abandon his post. He knows the kingdom is dead and its King has fallen into madness, but his oath contains no provision for either.

His three Gate Seals represent the kingdom's ancient defenses. As each seal breaks, challengers pass symbolically from the outside world into the King's buried memories.

#### Encounter lesson

Basic targeting, area damage, stagger, and Health-threshold phases.

#### Narrative purpose

Garran asks the central question of the band: does loyalty remain virtuous when its object becomes monstrous?

## Floor 2 — The Bloodwing Roost

### Velka, the Bloodwing Huntress

Velka was the King's royal huntmaster. She once hunted beasts threatening the kingdom; later, she was ordered to hunt deserters, rebels, and anyone accused of treason.

She still calls every battle "the hunt" because acknowledging her victims as people would mean confronting what she became.

#### Encounter lesson

Bleed management, pressure against weakened characters, and preventing an injured boss from recovering.

#### Narrative purpose

Velka shows the first stage of the King's decline: legitimate authority becoming persecution.

## Floor 3 — The Hollow Menagerie

### Morrowmaw, Broodkeeper

Morrowmaw was the prized monster of the royal menagerie. During the kingdom's final years, prisoners and war dead were thrown into its enclosure. When the palace fell silent, Morrowmaw continued breeding beneath it.

Its Broodlings contain fragments of those it consumed. Occasionally they whisper names erased from the royal histories.

> Proposed change: Rename the current floor from **The Third Vow** to **The Hollow Menagerie**, unless the Third Vow already has important lore elsewhere.

#### Encounter lesson

Summons, target prioritization, and denying a boss resources it can consume.

#### Narrative purpose

Morrowmaw represents institutional decay—the court continued feeding its appetites long after it had forgotten their purpose.

## Floor 4 — The Mirrored Hall

### Vaelor, the Mirrorbound

Vaelor served as the King's oracle. His mirrors did not show the future; they showed every future that might occur. For every loyal face, the King saw a reflection in which that person betrayed him.

Vaelor warned that possibility was not certainty. The King eventually shattered the oracle and bound him inside the mirrors, then continued consulting the fragments.

#### Encounter lesson

Mixing Physical and Magical damage, controlling burst, and respecting reflection windows.

#### Narrative purpose

Vaelor reveals how the King's fear became paranoia. The mirrors did not lie—the King simply chose to believe the worst reflection.

## Floor 5 — The First Warden's Bastion

### Kharad, the First Warden

Kharad was not appointed by the Mad King. He predates the kingdom and serves the Tower itself. His purpose is to prevent unworthy rulers, armies, and champions from reaching the higher bands.

The King passed Kharad's trial long ago. Kharad now considers that judgment his greatest failure.

#### Encounter lesson

Destructible objectives, barriers, stagger coordination, and preventing permanent boss escalation.

#### Narrative purpose

Kharad separates the palace's outer memories from its buried truth. Defeating him proves the party can confront what lies above.

## Floor 6 — The Ashen Belfry

### Orsenn, the Ashen Bellkeeper

Orsenn rang the palace bell for births, coronations, victories, and deaths. His final command was to ring for the Queen's funeral—but the King refused to declare her dead.

Unable to complete the toll, Orsenn has rung the same unfinished funeral for centuries. Every victim marked by his Cinder adds another note to it.

#### Encounter lesson

Tracking accumulating stacks and surviving or preventing a delayed detonation.

#### Narrative purpose

This is the first direct revelation of the event that broke the court: the death of the Queen.

## Floor 7 — The Endless Spring

### Eydis, the Endless Spring

Eydis tended the sacred waters beneath the palace. The King commanded her to restore the Queen to life. Eydis warned that the spring could preserve flesh but could not return a departed soul.

The King forced her to continue. The ritual merged Eydis with the spring, leaving both incapable of dying and incapable of creating true life.

#### Encounter lesson

Overcoming scaling regeneration, controlling a soft enrage, and balancing offense against defensive disruption.

#### Narrative purpose

Eydis represents denial. The King could have mourned, but instead tried to make loss itself illegal.

## Floor 8 — The Poisoned Vessel

### Kodoku, the Poisoned Vessel

Kodoku was created to contain every poison known to the royal apothecaries. After the Queen died, traces of Kodoku's venom were found in her blood.

The evidence never established whether Kodoku was the murder weapon, an attempted cure, or something introduced after her death. The King did not wait for certainty. He used the accusation to purge the court.

#### Encounter lesson

Poison, healing suppression, summon escalation, and sustained pressure across a larger party.

#### Narrative purpose

Kodoku introduces uncertainty. The player learns that the King's defining act was not discovering the truth—it was choosing the version that justified his wrath.

## Floor 9 — The Ninefold Court

### Ni, the Ninefold

Ni was the King's closest counsellor and spymaster. Ordered to identify the Queen's killer, Ni divided into nine bodies so each could pursue a different theory.

Every copy reached a different conclusion:

1. The Queen was murdered by a rival kingdom.
2. Vaelor arranged her death.
3. Eydis's ritual killed her.
4. Kodoku's poison killed her.
5. The Queen took her own life.
6. She died from an ordinary illness.
7. The King accidentally caused her death.
8. Ni caused her death.
9. There was never a murder.

Ni no longer remembers which body was the original—or whether one of the nine invented the others to escape execution.

#### Encounter lesson

Managing numerous inert objectives, controlling boss scaling, and preventing Health-swapping recovery.

#### Narrative purpose

Ni is the perfect penultimate guardian because the Ninefold Court denies the player a simple answer. The mystery matters less than what the King chose to do with it.

## Floor 10 — The Mad King's Throne

### The Mad King

The King waits upon a throne surrounded by the weapons of those he condemned. He presents himself as a grieving husband betrayed by everyone he trusted.

That grief is real—but so are his choices.

He ordered the hunts. He filled the menagerie. He imprisoned the oracle. He forbade the Queen's funeral. He corrupted the spring and purged his court. Whether the Queen was murdered cannot absolve what he did afterward.

His combat style contains no elegant royal magic. By the time challengers reach him, only rage, bloodlust, and the refusal to die remain.

#### Encounter lesson

A direct final damage and survival check after the more puzzle-oriented preceding floors.

#### Narrative purpose

The Mad King is both victim and villain. Defeating him does not solve the Queen's death; it ends the tyranny he built around that unanswered question.

## Encounter Progression

The existing roster forms a deliberate mechanical curriculum:

| Floor | Primary lesson |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Health thresholds and stagger |
| 2 | Bleed and weakened-target pressure |
| 3 | Summons and resource denial |
| 4 | Damage types and reflection |
| 5 | Objectives, barriers, and coordination |
| 6 | Stacking detonations |
| 7 | Scaling sustain and soft enrage |
| 8 | Healing denial and empowered summons |
| 9 | Add management and boss recovery |
| 10 | Final survival and damage check |

## Connection to Floors 11–20

The Court of the Broken Crown and the Stolen Hours share a thematic progression.

The Mad King could not accept one death and destroyed his kingdom trying to undo it. Aevum takes that same desire to its ultimate conclusion, attempting to abolish endings entirely by imprisoning a kingdom within a single hour.
